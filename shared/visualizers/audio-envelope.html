Why Sounds Need An Envelope

Loudness over time, and the click you get without it. Used in: web I-L8 · python I-L8 · c++ I-L10

A game sound is two things: a tone, and a shape. The tone is which note. The shape is how its loudness changes over the next tenth of a second — and the shape is what makes a sound feel like a coin, a thud or an explosion. Leave it out and every sound starts with an audible click, for a reason you can see on this page.

What to look for The top graph is loudness over time; the bottom one is the actual wave that reaches the speaker. Press Step 1 frame and follow the playhead. Now switch off the envelope: the wave starts at full height instantly, and that vertical jump is a click — a speaker cone being yanked rather than pushed. Then make the attack very long and watch the same note turn from a pluck into a swell, with no change to the note itself.
